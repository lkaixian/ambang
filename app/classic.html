      How the score is built

      One ratio of pressure to capacity, built from open Malaysian data. Every indicator is labelled real or illustrative here and on each destination page.

      SDG 14 Life Below WaterSDG 12 Responsible Consumption and ProductionSDG 8 Decent Work and Economic Growth (extension)

      The index

      AMBANG = 100 × P / (P + C)
      P · Pressure

      Weighted geometric mean of six indicators, each scaled 0 to 1: visitors, accommodation, hotel arrivals, cost of living, seasonality and occupancy.

      C · Capacity

      Geometric mean of the capacity indicators, equal-weighted today: waste handling, water headroom, resident base and live coral cover (river water quality for Cameron Highlands).

      50 · Threshold

      At 50, pressure equals what the place can absorb. Above it, the load exceeds capacity.

      BandRangeWhat it meansIndicated response
      Normal< 45Comfortable headroomPromote; absorb demand redirected from stressed sites
      Warning45 to 60Threshold VicinityMonitor monthly; prepare staged permit release
      Alert60 to 75Load exceeds capacityApply quota or pricing; prioritise infrastructure
      Critical75 and upSustained exceedanceConsider closure, moratorium, ecological assessment

      Pressure weights

      Entropy Weight Method: an indicator that varies more across destinations carries more discriminating information and earns more weight. Higher weight means more discriminating, not more important in a policy sense.

      Caveat: trained on a mix of real and illustrative values, so treat the weights as a working pipeline, not a finding.

      How the weights were trained

      python scripts/train_weights.py, n = 15 destinations × 6 indicators. Re-run on 2026-09-22 after marine park visitors became real data for the 14 marine islands: its weight rose from 22.9% to 45.3%, because the real spread between islands (Kedah's near 0.03, Terengganu's near 0.95) is far wider than the old placeholders. A hand-set 3/2/1 priority scheme was used before there were enough destinations to train on.

      Design choices

      Why a geometric mean

      So one binding constraint, such as no sewage treatment on a small island, cannot be offset by abundance elsewhere. Each destination names its lowest capacity indicator as its binding constraint, the same bottleneck logic (Liebig's law of the minimum) used by tourism carrying-capacity frameworks such as UNWTO/UNEP PAP-RAC and Cifuentes.

      Why entropy weights, and not a supervised model or expert panel

      The score is derived from these same indicators, so there is no independent target to train a model against; fitting one would only fit noise back onto itself. AHP or Delphi expert weighting needs external panels and several consensus rounds, which do not fit a hackathon timeline. Published Malaysian AHP weights exist for Perhentian and Tioman (Nasir, 2022) and are used as the reference in the capacity research report.

      Why capacity weights are equal

      Most capacity indicators are state-level, so every island in a state shares the same value. Entropy weighting would give those near-zero weight and reward only coral cover, which is the wrong way round for a weakest-link design. The research report proposes domain weights (ecological 0.35, infrastructure 0.45, resident 0.20) as the next step; they are our assumptions and are not applied yet.

      Normalisation

      Bounds are computed across the pooled destination-year panel and stored, so new years are scored against fixed history instead of rescaling the past.

      Per-island pressure is apportioned

      Visitor pressure is published at state level. Island figures are scaled from each state's total, so read them as relative ordering, not independent measurements.

      Visitor counts stop at 2017

      The scored islands use the Department of Marine Park Malaysia series, which ends in 2017 on the open portal. The score describes relative pressure, not today.

      Most capacity is state-level

      Waste, water and resident base are the same for every island in a state. Only live coral cover is measured per island.

      Resident base is weak

      Population is closer to a pressure measure than a capacity one. It stays for now because it is real and comparable; the research report suggests dropping or moving it.

      Weights are partly trained on illustration

      The entropy weights use a mix of real and illustrative values, so they will shift as more indicators become real.

      A band is a prompt, not a verdict

      Alert means a place warrants investigation, not that an ecological determination was made. Official statistics lag, so the newest score describes the latest observable year.

      Provisional scores are different

      They compare visitors with a daily limit, not the six-indicator score, and the limits disagree between sources. Never compare them with the scored islands.

      Interest is not visits

      The Seasonality calendar shows when people look places up. Most curves are nearly flat, so it says little about a visiting season.

      Language changes the answer

      Malaysians search in Malay ("Pulau Redang"); English terms mostly show foreign travellers, and the two differ a lot in size and season. We sum English and Malay Wikipedia views and ask for Malay and English Google Trends terms in one chart. An English-only curve would understate local interest.

      Corrections and rejected data

      • Payar, Lembu and Kaca map pins were about 1 degree of longitude too far west (99.0 instead of 100.04), in the sea beyond Langkawi. Moved to the Atlas coordinates, which agree with the 2014 polygon file.
      • "Besar" is Pulau Babi Besar, not another Pulau Besar: the Reef Check "Pulau Besar" location sits within 1 km of the Atlas point for Babi Besar.
      • "42 marine parks" is the federal count: 39 in Terengganu, Pahang, Kedah and Johor plus 3 in Labuan. The Atlas lists 51 protected areas, 46 of them designated Marine Park, so use 42 only for the federal Marine Park Department.
      • Atlas page counts for Sabah and Sarawak (5 and 3) differ from its own list (7 and 2). Neither is quoted.
      • Kuala Terengganu district was left out for Redang and its neighbours: the 1994 gazette names a district that has since been split, so its figures would describe the wrong place.
      • "Only 100 visitors a day" (May 2020) was a COVID rule for all Sabah parks, not a carrying capacity, so it is not used for any score.
      • 548,598 visitors to 56 islands (Kosmo, June 2026) has no stated year and no per-island split. It is context only.
      • State constituency data (DOSM publishes it) was checked and rejected: the Pangkor and Tioman constituencies include mainland towns, so they would overstate the islands.
      • Sembilang map pin was about 22 km east of the gazetted Pulau Sembilang (104.10 instead of 103.89); moved to the Atlas point.
      • OpenStreetMap accommodation zeros are not trusted: Rawa has a resort that OSM does not tag, so a zero may only mean untagged. Islands with a zero keep their placeholder bar instead of scoring as having no accommodation.
      • Google Trends seasonality was tested as the Seasonality bar and rejected: search interest is flat across months (about 10 to 12 effective months everywhere) and misses the monsoon closures, so real monthly visits from the Marine Park Department are used instead.

      What would raise data quality most

      • Department of Marine Park Malaysia: per-island visitor counts for 2018 to 2025 and any daily limits. Would replace the state-level series and the 2017 cut-off.
      • Sabah Parks: the carrying-capacity study behind the 2019 visitor-limit panel. Would let Tun Sakaran and Pulau Tiga be scored (their visitor counts are already real).
      • Sarawak Forestry Corporation: visitor counts for Miri-Sibuti and Talang-Satang, none of which is public.
      • Island sewage and waste: published only as news and one resort survey (Perhentian), never as data. Needs a request to the state governments.

      Which places are covered

      Scored (15)

      The marine parks of Peninsular Malaysia that Reef Check actually surveys, in four clusters, plus Cameron Highlands as the highland comparison.

      • Terengganu: Perhentian, Redang, Lang Tengah, Kapas
      • Pahang: Tioman, Tulai, Sembilang
      • Kedah: Payar, Lembu, Kaca
      • Johor: Sibu, Tinggi, Besar, Rawa
      • Highland: Cameron Highlands

      Going island-level is limited by ecological data, not by choice: Johor's park has roughly 20 islands but only four have a coral-cover series.

      Provisional score (3)

      Real visitor numbers compared with a daily limit. Different method; not comparable with the scored islands.

        • Pangkor: no usable visitor series
        • Fraser's Hill: no usable visitor series; first set aside for low volume, now tracked as a second highland
        • Miri: Sarawak data sit behind a manual download
        Not tracked
        • Selangor, Penang: no marine park, no Reef Check reef, no SWCorp waste column
        • Genting Highlands: casino and resort-led; set aside
        • Labuan: gazetted, no data verified yet
